## Hot take debate cues · 3:00 · slide `s-hot-take`

**Motion:** NYC's one-year AI moratorium is a mistake.

**Henry (against):**
- Classroom ban outsources AI literacy to families; not all families are equal.
- About 600K students lose school-supervised access; students without home alternatives fall furthest behind.
- Schools should teach supervised use, judgment, and verification — not remove the tool.
- Mind-changer: evidence the pause improves outcomes and equity without widening the private-access gap.

**Andy (for the scoped pause):**
- One-year moratorium through grade 8 with exceptions; not a universal ban.
- A time-limited pause lets procurement, privacy rules, and evidence catch up.
- Attention and human teaching have developmental value in the K-8 window.
- Mind-changer: evidence supervised classroom AI improves learning equitably without dependence or privacy harm.

**Verdict (read explicitly):** The moratorium is the wrong default. Restricting classroom access doesn't restrict access — it restricts *equitable* access. The right answer is supervised use, judgment, verification instruction, and privacy-protective procurement.
