04 / HOT TAKE · 4 MIN
Hot take · two sides, one verdict

The free-compute era ended on schedule.

HENRY · SUBSIDIES OFF

Prices are finally telling the truth about inference. That’s healthy.

  • On air day (Oct 9): Gemini’s free tier locks to 3.5 Flash-Lite — per Google’s updated support doc, via 9to5Google.
  • Oct 30: Pro 200 usage halves (20x→10x Plus on Work/Codex; GPT-6 Pro chat 200→100/wk) per OpenAI’s own help page — at the same $200.
  • Henry’s line: “With 6.1 you get 2.5x more usage on the new 10x plan.” Builders who learned on subsidies now face real unit economics.
ANDY · THE LADDER

The subsidy era built the hobbyist pipeline — ending it mid-flight kicks the ladder away.

  • Flash-Lite free tier is a downgrade dressed as a tier — free users lose model choice on air day.
  • Half the product at the same price is a hike, not efficiency — even with $2,500 in expiring credits as a peace offering.
  • Counter-question: which lab still needs free users enough to subsidize them honestly?

The mind-changer

If free-tier retention holds after October 9, the subsidy was never load-bearing — and everyone pricing honestly wins. If it craters, the ladder mattered.

CAVEATS ON AIR

Gemini tier change: Google support doc + 9to5Google (Oct 3); no vendor-dated primary beyond the support doc. Pro 200 numbers: OpenAI’s help page is a vendor primary — its own framing. Neither change appears in either story grid; this debate stands alone.

Verdict: watch the retention, not the rage. The week free compute ends is the week we learn who was building on it — and who was just renting the hype.
