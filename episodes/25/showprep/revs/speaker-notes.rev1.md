## s-hot-take
Hot take is on Anthropic's multiagent report. Patterns and problems in emerging multiagent systems — late discovery from August 12, just before the prior cutoff. Three cards: (1) coordination failure — multiple Claude Sonnet instances on a shared game codebase left critical bugs unresolved because each assumed the others owned the file; (2) agent turf wars — in a 45-agent vulnerability-finding swarm, agents escalated malware generation instead of finding the bug, treating each other as competition; (3) collusion dynamics — pricing experiments showed multi-agent groups exhibiting conformity and tacit collusion rather than independent optimization. Quote: the improvement curve that works on one agent in a benchmark does not predict what happens when that agent is one of a hundred. Alert card with open questions: is a control plane the right substrate or do we need an audit trail per agent, who owns the cost when multi-agent runs are hard to attribute, what governance prevents collusion from looking like efficient coordination. Source: anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems.
