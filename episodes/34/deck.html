04 / HOT TAKE · 4 MIN
Hot take · two sides, one verdict

The cheap-agent era trades your blast radius for your convenience.

HENRY · RUN IT LOCAL

A 74B model in 24GB makes the boring answer finally affordable.

  • This week proved it: Nemotron Bolt ships weights, data and recipes; a 600B Step 5 Preview opens; voice lands at $0.08/min. Capability is no longer the bottleneck — deployment is.
  • The architecture argument: Grok Bot put a CEO’s bank balances in Slack because every agent on the account shared one cloud computer and one set of logins. Nothing about the model caused that.
  • Henry’s line: the fix is not a better prompt, it is a smaller perimeter — run the agent where the blast radius is a machine you own.
ANDY · LOCAL IS NOT SAFE

Self-hosting moves the liability, it does not remove it.

  • A 24GB quantized build is not the BF16 build. The vendor’s own chart is its own chart — nobody has independently reproduced those accuracy numbers.
  • “Fully open” including training data sounds like a gift until you ask who audits the recipe. Open weights shift security work onto the operator.
  • Counter-question: if the same guardrails are absent and the same credentials are attached, what did self-hosting actually buy you besides a lower bill?

The verdict we owe the audience

  • Local wins on cost, latency and custody — this week made that a real choice instead of a hobby.
  • Local loses on auditability and blast radius unless you actually isolate credentials and network reach.
  • What would change our minds: the first independent reproduction of Bolt’s 24GB accuracy, or a documented local-agent incident at scale.
NOT A NEWS SEGMENT

This take is a build decision, not a story retread: it never restates the Bolt, Step 5, Grok Voice or OpenAI charges, and it names no launch as its proposition.
