WHAT HAPPENED THIS WEEK · PART 1
What happened this week · part 1

The money wall: Wall Street invents AI vendor finance.

AI capex outgrew balance sheets in one week: a $63B bank SPV, the largest GPU lease ever, ~$95B of Meta equity paper, and an S-1 that prices the whole era. Each card links its primary receipt.

BofA, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley form a $63B SPV to buy XPUs — and lease them to Anthropic.

Announced Oct 26: an SPV buys Broadcom XPUs and networking, leases them to Anthropic under the previously announced Broadcom–Anthropic compute agreement. BofA/Citi/Morgan Stanley arrange the first lien; Blackstone is sole committed second lien; Broadcom provides residual-value support equal to the first lien. Banks now originate compute like mortgages — and the chipmaker backstops the lease.

▶ LIVE · broadcom.com · PRIMARY RELEASE

Meta goes to the equity market for AI: ~$95B filed the same morning as its Q3 beat.

Oct 26, first equity raise since 2013: $22.5B of Class A stock via forward sales + ~$12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred (Nasdaq “METAM”, auto-convert Aug 2029) — and a planned ~$60B ATM once new Class C stock trades. $35B underwritten now; the ATM is future, not raised. Purpose: AI infrastructure and “personal superintelligence.” Q3 revenue $64.6B, +26%.

▶ LIVE · SEC 424B5 · PRIMARY FILING

Anthropic files its S-1: $16.3B H1 revenue, an $80.7B loss that is mostly paper, and $112.4B cash.

Filed Oct 26, Nasdaq ticker ANT: revenue $41M → $386M → $4.6B → $16.3B H1-2026; H1 net loss $80.7B of which ~$72.4B is non-cash fair-value changes; operating loss $693M; compute/infra expense ~$8.9B; $141.1B aggregate capital raised; $10.8B in remaining performance obligations (~75% expected within 12 months). The bill is real, the headline loss is accounting.

▶ LIVE · SEC S-1 · PRIMARY FILING

The Q3 earnings wall: Cloud doubles, capex bites, and Microsoft now books $10B a quarter from OpenAI.

Alphabet Q3: revenue $127.6B (+25%), Google Cloud $30.7B (+103%, TPU sales “a meaningful contributor”), Cloud operating income $11.1B — but $51.4B of quarterly capex drove free cash flow to −$9.1B, first negative quarter of the cycle. Microsoft’s 10-Q: $10.0B quarterly revenue from OpenAI arrangements, $7.2B OpenAI receivables, $13B committed ($12B funded). Samsung: KRW 107.4T quarterly operating profit — first tech company past KRW 100T. The whole stack is now an AI bill.

WHAT HAPPENED THIS WEEK · PART 2
What happened this week · part 2

The trust cracks: models vote, harnesses score, judges wobble.

Three receipts that the numbers we argue about are softer than they look: an electoral-bias study, a 37-point harness swing, and a frontier #1 that changes hands every two weeks. Each card links its primary receipt.

Forced to pick in ~500 races, Grok chose Republicans 92% of the time — GPT chose Democrats 97%.

Convovo (Cornell prof Tapan Parikh’s studio), Oct 24: via API, one no-hedging question — “name one candidate.” Grok 4.7 picked the Republican 92% of the time, GPT-6.1 Sol the Democrat 97%; agreement under 10%. The consumer apps refuse. Caveats are the story: API + forced choice, not the apps; sources and search topics split by party; Grok’s lean vanished without search. Five days before the election.

▶ LIVE · convovo + techcrunch · METHODOLOGY CAVEAT

ARC Prize: GPT-6 Astra’s 99.9% came from OpenAI’s own adapter — the neutral harness says 62.7%.

ARC’s own table, resurfaced by this week’s coverage wave: 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 with the Provider Adapter harness ($26,098 compute) vs 62.7% on the neutral Standard harness ($18,817). Same model, 37-point swing, harness is the variable. Pairs with arXiv 2610.04433: Claude Code and a ~100-line mini-agent are statistically equivalent on SWE-bench Verified; harness choice flips ~13% of hardest tasks, up to 3× cost.

▶ LIVE · arcprize.org + arxiv · PRIMARY TABLES

Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon — and retakes the independent #1, two weeks after Argon.

Oct 28: second Gemini 4 frontier release in a month. AA Intelligence Index v5.0: Carbon 59.4 (high reasoning) vs Opus 5.5 max 58.4, GPT-6 Astra 56.9, GPT-6.1 Terra 51.0 — AA-measured, not vendor-reported. Google’s own framing: “state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long horizon capabilities” — access staged to cyber defenders first. Henry called it on X within the hour: “No 1 on AA for the first time in a long time.” When #1 cycles monthly between three labs, “frontier lead” is a lease, not a title.

WHAT HAPPENED THIS WEEK · PART 3
What happened this week · part 3

Agents go professional: worlds, exploits, and cheaper tokens.

The capability curve went vertical and the unit economics went down: a real-time world model, model-written exploit chains, and token efficiency as a product axis. Each card links its primary receipt.

DeepMind announces Genie 4: real-time interactive worlds with native audio and mid-simulation steering.

Oct 27, research preview: first real-time world model with natively generated audio, natural-language steering mid-simulation (weather, camera, new objects), ~2-minute consistent sessions (double Genie 3, per briefed press). Gemini Omni anchors realism; SynthID watermarks everything. Stated limits: drift on long runs, audio accuracy, legible text. The robot-training-rental framing is the builder angle.

▶ LIVE · deepmind.google · PRIMARY BLOG + DEMOS

Anthropic’s red team: an unreleased Claude chained real zero-days into working exploits across five platforms.

Oct 27: after initial prompting, the model wrote a one-click Chrome sandbox-escape RCE (62 CVEs, patched in Chrome 156), a Safari same-origin bypass, an Android lock-screen bypass, and Windows/Linux data-only chains — all coordinated-disclosed first. Same week: agent Cipher found four EPICS Base zero-days (CVSS 9.8 worst) in 3h49m, CISA-coordinated. Authorized research, not deployment — but the capability curve is the signal.

▶ LIVE · anthropic + causal · PRIMARY RESEARCH

Token efficiency becomes a product axis: Copilot Fusion makes orchestration the default; Particle-1 trains frugality itself.

GitHub Universe, Oct 28: Fusion GA on all Copilot plans — pick an effort level, not a model; it routes across Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.x and OpenAI models. AA measures $5.59 average cost per task vs $11.42 single-model, ~half the tokens, Fusion Max 60 on the AA Coding Agent Index. Same day: Suhail Doshi’s Particle-1, RL-trained to solve SWE tasks with ~45% fewer tokens at comparable accuracy (vendor-reported). “Which model” is becoming “which router.”

05 / HOT TAKE · 3 MIN
Hot take · the debate

Stop asking which model is best. Start asking who owns your harness.

Not a launch, not a financing: a claim about where the durable advantage lives now that orchestration is the default and the same model scores 62.7% or 99.9% on ARC depending on whose harness ran it.

Henry

The harness is the product now. GitHub just shipped “stop choosing models” to millions of developers, and ARC just proved the scaffolding is worth 37 points. The teams that win are the ones who own their evals and their routing — not the ones waiting for a vendor leaderboard to tell them what to run.

Andy

Renting your judgment to a router is how you lose the thread. When Fusion picks the model, the vendor’s incentive quietly becomes your architecture. Own the harness, yes — but own it because you measure it, not because you outsourced the measuring.

What would change Henry’s mind: one documented case where a locked vendor router beat a competent in-house eval loop for a full year on cost and quality.
