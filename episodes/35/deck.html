WHAT HAPPENED THIS WEEK · PART 1
What happened this week · part 1

Open models won the benchmark and lost the catalogue.

Two open launches cleared the frontier bar this week. Four days later the federal register and Google Cloud each published a removal. Each card links its primary receipt.

DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash-1017: an open model that beats GPT-5.6 Sol on Terminal-Bench.

MIT-licensed, 1M context, repo live 2026-10-17T02:40Z. Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 42.3 vs Sol 39.9 and GLM-5.3 37.9; Opus 5 still ahead at 51.8. The same weekend it opened a Search & Fetch API at $0.75 per 1,000 calls, free inside Claude Code and Codex until Nov 1.

▶ LIVE · huggingface.co · VENDOR BENCH, MIT WEIGHTS

MiniMax launches M3.1 Flash — and reveals Space Bunny was it all along.

MiniMax says the anonymous OpenRouter model that processed 200+ trillion tokens was an early M3.1 Flash. 73.7% DeepSWE v1.1, 82% CyberGym, $0.15/$0.60 per million tokens. Weights are promised “later” — not open yet.

▶ LIVE · minimax.io · VENDOR BENCH, WEIGHTS PENDING

DHS orders DeepSeek removed from every civilian federal system.

Federal Register notice 2026-21447, published Oct 20: exclusion and removal across FCEB IT and procurement. Vendors now have to prove no transitive DeepSeek routing. Reuters and The Verge reported it a day earlier.

▶ LIVE · federalregister.gov · PRIMARY ORDER

Google Cloud adds OpenAI’s models and retires all 16 open-model endpoints the same day.

Oct 21: GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Luna land in Model Garden private preview. The same day’s deprecation notice retires every managed open-model endpoint — DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, Llama, MiniMax, gpt-oss. Open weights lose their easiest enterprise home.

WHAT HAPPENED THIS WEEK · PART 2
What happened this week · part 2

The compute bill and the moat under it.

The leaked prospectus priced the closed-model era, and the same week three separate layers of NVIDIA’s software moat got attacked. Each card links its primary receipt.

Anthropic’s draft S-1: $16.3B revenue, $80.7B loss, and up to $750B in compute commitments.

WSJ review of the draft prospectus, Oct 20: H1 revenue $16.3B from $1.1B, net loss $80.7B of which ~$72B is non-cash convertible-note accounting, ~$9B of real H1 infra spend, and up to $750B of possible commitments including non-cancellable $111B Google, $110B AWS, $31B Microsoft. Two customers are ~20% of revenue. Investors weighing ~$1.5T against ~$2T.

▶ LIVE · WSJ + Reuters review · DRAFT DOC, NOT FILED

Amazon and Google open PyTorch for Trainium and TPU, and Tenstorrent raises $1.5B at $6.2B.

TorchNeuron is Apache-2.0 and ports a model with one .to('neuron') call; TorchTPU is open source with Meta co-development and native vLLM/SGLang engines in production at Meta. Tenstorrent closed a $1.5B Series E at $6.2B on Oct 19. China’s three biggest platforms ordered 3M+ RTX PRO 5500s, $35B+, more than Nvidia planned to make.

WHAT HAPPENED THIS WEEK · PART 3
What happened this week · part 3

Agents went to war — and the keyring got hit.

Three receipts from one week of agentic operations: an attacker’s unit economics, a compromised gateway package, and the first credible account of agents running production incident response. Each card links its primary receipt.

One hacker’s agents broke into 100+ companies for about $50 a breach.

Reuters and NorForge, Oct 19: a Chinese-speaking operator pointed Cairn agents at 1,200+ sites from Sep 25; 100+ breached, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. Roughly 23 hours of unsupervised probing per success, a few thousand dollars of tokens. The Army is buying the same thing: ARCYBER’s ECHOTRIBBLE, still whitepaper-phase.

▶ LIVE · reuters + intercept · PRIMARY REPORT

A bad LiteLLM wheel on PyPI went after the keyring — and Railway froze every deploy.

Attackers stole GitHub CLI OAuth tokens from three LiteLLM maintainers, built 1.107.0 from a lookalike repo, and shipped a credential stealer plus an hourly backdoor. Caught and pulled the same day. Railway disclosed the same week: encrypted env vars accessed, some decrypted, all deployments paused, upstream AI keys revoked — “a series of attacks targeting platform companies to compromise AI inference providers.”

▶ LIVE · github + railway · PRIMARY DISCLOSURES

xAI’s Grok Bot cleared 204 support tickets in ten minutes — with one human approval.

IncidentWatch scans the support queue every five minutes; IncidentSweep batches the affected tickets and closes them only after an engineer approves. In the Sept 18 incident it cleared 204 tickets in about ten minutes. Vendor-reported numbers, and the approval gate is the whole design.

05 / HOT TAKE · 3 MIN
Hot take · the debate

Taste is the last moat. Everything else got automated.

Not a launch, not a breach, not a valuation: a claim about what is actually scarce now that a 2.5B-active open model writes production code and a $0.15-per-million API closes tickets.

Henry

If code is cheap, the scarce input is knowing what to build and what to throw away. The people who win the next two years are the ones with judgment about the product, not the ones with the fastest fingers. That is why the model race stopped being the interesting question.

Andy

Taste without distribution is a hobby. The moat is the boring stuff: who holds the keys, who carries the pager, who can prove the thing works. This week’s $50-a-breach story is a taste problem only if you have never been paged at 3am.

What would change Henry’s mind: one documented case where a taste-led, non-engineer team shipped and kept a production system for a year.
