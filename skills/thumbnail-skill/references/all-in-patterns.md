# All-In thumbnail and title observations

The historical 2026-08-08 sample below illustrates one listing-title style. Compare current relevant examples when useful; distinguish observed wording from evidence of performance. Do not treat this sample as a required formula or as proof of thumbnail effectiveness:

- `Chip Stocks Crash, $20B Fund Margin Called, Frontier Labs: SLOW DOWN AI, Mamdani's Grocery Stores`
- `The Fight Over Open Source AI, Anthropic's $1.5B Payout, NYC Socialists: Evictions = Violence?`
- `Can the AI Industry Regulate Itself? Stripe Wants PayPal, China Catches Up, NY Bans Datacenters`
- `OpenAI vs Anthropic IPOs, Anthropic $3T, Zuck's Price War, China Ends Open Source?, Trump Accounts`
- `AI Sovereignty Wars, Palantir-Nvidia Deal, SCOTUS Birthright Ruling, Newsom's CA Budget Lie`

These examples often lead with a concrete story or question and stack other topics. That is one option, not a requirement: use the approach that best communicates the actual episode and interests Weekly Claw viewers.

- Keep a stable brand anchor, usually the logo and bold type.
- Change the main idea per episode: guest-led portraits, four-person host wall, single speaker crop, topical scene, or a high-contrast text hook.
- Use large faces and one readable canonical episode title; avoid dense summary copy.
- Let palette and mood change with the story. Dark/purple, black/yellow, and brighter guest-led treatments can all still feel like one show.
- Recurring visual grammar matters more than repeating the exact arrangement.
- Recent thumbnails make the people the composition: large side-by-side or wall-of-faces crops fill most of the frame, with short two-to-five-word text over the lower third or clean negative space rather than a separate half-canvas text panel.
- The thumbnail and listing can use complementary wording; both must honestly represent the episode. Choose numbering according to established Weekly Claw practice or explicit user direction.
- Use a topic ledger to find specific, verifiable stories, but select the lead for viewer interest and support in the recording, not airtime alone. Follow [title development](title-development.md).
- Once likeness references and three composition directions are fixed, use GPT Image 2 for the final shared-scene base images. Add typography and brand marks deterministically after generation.
- For recurring two-host episodes, default to both current-episode hosts side by side at comparable visual weight. Keep the background quiet; do not add detailed machinery, literal world-building, or a separate title card merely to create variation.

Apply this as a Weekly Claw principle: consistent identity, deliberate episode-specific variation.
