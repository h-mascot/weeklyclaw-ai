# Weekly Claw Thumbnail Skill

Create a distinct, mobile-readable thumbnail for each Weekly Claw episode. Preserve a recognisable show grammar without cloning the prior episode.

## Linked guest and release routing — 2026-10-04.1

When the current episode includes a guest, first read [references/guest-roster-adaptation.md](references/guest-roster-adaptation.md). It scopes the historical two-host prompt to the actual roster without changing historical identity evidence or bypassing generation/approval gates.

Receive the exact candidate and topic/roster record from the [episode editor](../weekly-claw-episode-editor/SKILL.md). Keep thumbnail composition separate from its full-frame camera and global programme-mark rules. After exact selection, pass the image hash, approval scope, matching master and separate `thumbnail_headline` and `episode_title` values to the [publisher](../weekly-claw-youtube-publisher/SKILL.md). A thumbnail-wording revision or candidate approval changes only `thumbnail_headline`; preserve the full `episode_title` unless the user explicitly changes the listing title. This distinction adds no approval step. Creating or approving the image does not mean it has been applied to the correct video; the publisher verifies the remote custom thumbnail and requested visibility. Existing valid selection or scoped user acceptance remains valid for unchanged bytes.

## Canonical host identity lock

Before generation, obtain the current approved host identity pack, photographs, generation prompt and provenance through the authorized private workflow. These personal assets are intentionally not included in this public bundle. Verify hashes, availability, authorization and current-episode references. Treat approved real identity anchors as higher authority than generated candidates, and use current footage for removable equipment. Never substitute reconstructed or generated images for missing approved photographs. Stop for an authorized replacement if required identity evidence is unavailable.

Reuse recorded authorization for generated likenesses when its scope covers the current work. Approval to use a photograph alone does not establish that scope; preserve the separate generation and exact-candidate approval records.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the episode’s real topic, participants, existing brand assets, and the most recently approved thumbnail before designing. Apply the canonical host identity lock above: approved user-designated cards govern facial anatomy, hashed real cross-episode references support likeness, and current-episode frames govern participant presence, clothing and removable equipment. For a guest without an approved identity pack, use verified current-episode frames.
2. Develop and compare distinct, source-grounded title and thumbnail-hook pairs using [title development](references/title-development.md). Record the strongest options, evidence, and selection rationale. Respect an exact user-supplied listing title; retain separate `episode_title` and `thumbnail_headline` values.
3. Define one visual idea and an episode-specific palette. Use the official mark with the claw icon before `WEEKLY`; colour the claw to fit the episode rather than forcing red. For every new generation/branding round, also place a small standalone Weekly Claw claw icon in the bottom-left safe margin. Keep current approved assets immutable. For recurring two-host episodes, make the current-episode hosts the composition: large, recognisable, side by side, and integrated into one continuous simple scene.
4. Generate three distinct 16:9 candidates for every initial concept and every revision round. Hold participants and brand anchors fixed; use up to three strong, distinct editorial hooks from the title exercise when appropriate, and vary meaningful composition choices such as crop, host spacing, title placement, or hierarchy; do not present three trivial colour swaps. After the directions and current-episode reference frames are verified, use GPT Image 2 as the final shared-scene generation pass for all three base images; if GPT Image 2 is unavailable, fall back to Gemini Nano Banana Pro (gemini-3-pro-image-preview via the ce-gemini-imagegen skill, GEMINI_API_KEY env). Do not stop at deterministic webcam crops, feathered source composites, or a generated background behind separate cards. Give the generator the approved identity cards and verified real-camera references plus current-episode context under the identity lock above, and explicitly constrain participant identity, glasses, headphones, clothing, count, composition, negative space, and forbidden artifacts. Generate the base scenes without text, logo, or badge, then apply the exact headline, official mark, and episode badge deterministically so spelling and branding remain exact. Faces must be natural, flattering, large, and recognisable; regenerate weak likenesses.
5. Keep the composition simple in the All-In podcast grammar: faces occupy most of the canvas, `thumbnail_headline` overlays the lower third or clean negative space, and the background stays quiet and tonal or uses a restrained studio gradient. Keep the episode badge wholly inside its container. Prefer no prop or one simple supporting object. Reject separate half-canvas text panels, elaborate literal environments, dense machinery, busy scene-building, noisy decorative layers, and tiny explanatory copy.
6. Run a mandatory independent QA pass before showing previews. The reviewer must compare all three candidates to the approved identity cards and verified real-camera references, current episode footage and participant list; use the most recently approved thumbnail for style only, never as a generated likeness reference; test each at both 1280×720 and 160×90; and independently assess the headline’s clarity, audience interest and episode support as well as likeness, clean eyeglass lenses, participant coverage, each candidate’s exact recorded `thumbnail_headline` text, logo order, badge containment, meaningful A/B/C variation, and mobile readability. For Andy, the likeness check must cover both geometry (broad, near-square, never oval) and soft-tissue identity (full fleshy cheeks, soft wide rounded chin, wide but softly padded jaw, dense dark fully-connected beard, medium-to-large nose, mid-30s regular-guy build, large head-to-shoulders ratio). Broad-but-generic is a FAIL. Regenerate any rejected candidate rather than presenting it for user approval.
7. Put only the three QA-passed preview images directly in the chat, label them A/B/C, and stop for selection and approval. Do not alter YouTube, the website, video, metadata, or published assets until the user explicitly approves one exact candidate.
8. After approval, bind the selection text to the exact path and SHA-256. Update the custom YouTube thumbnail and matching website asset only when the publishing workflow authorizes those surfaces; retain a versioned source image and verify every rendered result.

## Permanent draft archive

Never discard or overwrite a generated base, rejected draft, branded candidate, contact sheet, mobile derivative, prompt, QA record, or approved final. After every generation and deterministic branding pass, copy the complete round into `the authorized private archive for the run`, preserving descriptive versioned filenames and source-relative paths. Record SHA-256 values for every approval candidate. Keep the working originals in place; the archive is additive and must not become an identity-reference source.

## Design rules

- Make thumbnail wording concise and readable, and pair it with a complementary, accurate listing title. Choose length and format for the idea, not a mandatory word count or docket formula.
- Treat every on-screen participant as part of the episode. Equal presence is a sound default; vary hierarchy only when the episode’s idea genuinely needs it.
- Preserve a coherent show system through the logo, typography confidence, portrait quality, and clean editorial composition. Keep the standalone claw icon at bottom-left on new rounds, with safe margins and sufficient contrast at 160×90; never add it retroactively to an approved file without a new QA and approval round.
- Vary each episode through its editorial idea, palette, background, portrait arrangement, crop, or type layout. Candidates may test different well-grounded hooks; record the exact headline on each and preserve `episode_title` separately.
- Generate faces using the approved identity hierarchy and current-episode context above, with GPT Image 2 as the primary generator (Gemini Nano Banana Pro as fallback). Reject outputs with a wrong likeness, dim or unflattering face, small faces, or uncanny rendering.
- Reject Andy outputs that trend toward the generic handsome bald man: angular or chiselled jawline, hollow or sunken cheeks, pointed or narrow chin, sparse/patchy/wispy beard, small slim refined nose, leaner or older appearance. Cross-check facial anatomy against the approved user-designated card headshots and hashed real-photo references, never generated prior thumbnails. Do not silently substitute an unapproved identity source, and do not downgrade to deterministic source composites merely because they are easier to produce.
- Reject distracting window, screen, or ring-light reflections in eyeglass lenses. Keep the participant's real frame shape and make both eyes clearly visible.
- Cover the episode's on-screen podcast participants. For a two-person episode, both people must be visibly and recognisably represented unless the user explicitly directs otherwise.
- Do not default to a split portrait wall, three-card layout, or participants flanking a separate headline panel. For recurring two-host episodes, place both current-episode hosts side by side at comparable visual weight, give their faces most of the canvas, and overlay the title in the lower third or clean negative space. Vary interaction geometry, crop, palette, title placement and, where useful, the editorial hook; keep participant identity accurate. Use a card-based layout only when the user explicitly requests it or the episode concept requires it.
- Never invent a participant, logo, claim, episode topic, or sponsor.

## Required QA record

For Henry, record an explicit current-eyewear comparison and anatomy comparison (face length, eyes, nose, lips, beard volume/outline and skin tone) against the authoritative card and verified real frames. A generic overall “likeness PASS” is insufficient. Resolve ambiguity before presentation; the user's subsequent recognition/selection remains decisive.

Before a preview is returned, record a concise pass/fail result for: required All-In composition format, current-episode source grounding, correct participant count, each likeness (for Andy: broad near-square geometry AND soft-tissue identity — cheek fullness, beard density, nose size, chin shape, age, head-to-body — compared against the approved user-designated card headshots and hashed real-photo references), side-by-side face scale and spacing, clean eyeglass lenses, each candidate’s exact recorded `thumbnail_headline` wording, claw-before-`WEEKLY` mark order, themed accent colour, badge placement, meaningful variation across all three candidates and from adjacent episodes, and 160×90 readability. A failed or ambiguous item blocks the preview until regenerated. If Henry explicitly accepts an exact candidate with a known defect, record the candidate hash, acceptance text and remaining failed criterion; that scoped exception permits only the accepted use and must not be relabelled as an independent likeness PASS or applied to future episodes.
